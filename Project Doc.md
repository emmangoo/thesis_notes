Investigating Trans Regulatory Effects Leveraging Multi-Omics Outliers in Cancer

1. #  Motivation
    

Current outlier approaches focus heavily on cis-acting variants, local genetic changes that affect a single gene. However, this approach often fails to explain samples exhibiting "molecular chaos, which could arise in cancer. By shifting our focus to hyper-outlier samples, we can uncover systemic trans-regulatory failures. Understanding these effects is critical because it reveals how a single "hit" to a global regulator (like the spliceosome or a transcription factor) can destabilize hundreds of downstream targets. Identifying a trans-regulator as the root cause allows for targeting a single pathway to potentially "correct" a broad spectrum of molecular outliers.

8. # Core messages
    

While rare disease and cancer samples typically exhibit a baseline frequency of 3–10 molecular outliers per sample, we have identified a subset of "hyper-outlier" samples containing ~80 or more. These extreme deviations cannot be explained solely by local cis-regulatory variants. This project aims to demonstrate that these clusters are driven by systemic trans-regulatory disruptions, where a single upstream defect triggers a cascade of downstream molecular failures.
