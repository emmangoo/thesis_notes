## 1. Introduction

- **1.1 Motivation**
    - Cancer genomes show widespread molecular disruption (aberrant expression, splicing, protein level) that isn't always explained by a local (cis) variant on the affected gene current approaches focus on local explanations of variants, yet many tumours show molecular disruption across several genes spanning aberrant expression, splicing, or protein levels, citing Bailey, Tokheim, Porta-Pardo et al. (driver gene landscape paper — 🔎 get full citation).
    - Define cis vs trans effects precisely (variant directly on the gene vs. no local explanation — implies a distal/trans regulator) — this is your central conceptual framework (defense slides 3–4).
    - State the core hypothesis: disruption of _shared cellular machinery_ (spliceosome, CNV-driven dosage change, RNA Pol II) is a _trans_ mechanism that elevates outlier burden across many genes simultaneously, not just the mutated one.
- **1.2 Why RNA-seq-derived outliers are the right readout**
    - Rare disease/cancer diagnostics increasingly combine DNA-seq with RNA-seq because RNA "can help reveal splicing defects, mono-allelic expression of loss-of-function variants, and expression outliers" RNA-seq can help to reveal splicing defects, the mono-allelic expression of heterozygous loss-of-function variants, and expression outliers (OUTRIDER paper).
    - Motivate why coding-variant-only pipelines miss a lot: noncoding/regulatory variation is a substantial share of rare-disease diagnostic yield promoter variants account for 6% of the total diagnostic yield, which combined with the 14% attributable to cryptic splice variants implies noncoding variants account for roughly one in five cases (Promoter mutation deep-learning paper) — use this to justify why trans/regulatory mechanisms deserve dedicated study in cancer too.
- **1.3 Aims / Research Questions** (map 1:1 onto your three analysis notebooks)
    1. Do CNVs act as trans-regulators of genome-wide outlier burden, and can chromosome-aware statistics separate real trans-drivers from co-amplified passengers? (`significant_variants` notebook)
    2. Does disruption of core/minor spliceosome machinery (small variants) increase splicing outlier burden genome-wide? (`spliceosome_variants` notebook)
    3. Does RNA Polymerase II dysfunction drive genome-wide transcriptional dysregulation (over/underexpression), independent of background CNV burden and cancer lineage? (`transcrip_dysreg` notebook)
- **1.4 Contributions** — bullet list of what's novel: chromosome-aware burden testing method, the two-part hurdle framework for zero-inflated multi-omic overlap outliers, pathway-agnostic replication of a single-gene finding (CDK4/CDK6), cross-omic Pol II analysis with explicit removal of circularity.

- **3.4 Statistical framework** (organize as a "menu" the reader can refer back to — you used ~8 different tests, be explicit about _why_ each one was tried and _why_ it was replaced)
    
    1. **Global rank-sum (Wilcoxon/Mann–Whitney-style z-test) with FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg)** — carriers of a CNV in gene X ranked against the global burden distribution; z computed via standard rank-sum expectation/variance formula, not scipy's implementation (custom vectorized version for genome-wide scan speed) — explain this choice explicitly, including why exact Mann–Whitney U wasn't used at scale.
    2. **Chromosome-aware version of the same test** — same test, but the sample's total burden used for ranking excludes outliers on the same chromosome as the CNV gene, removing local/passenger contributions.
    3. **Cohen's d filter** (|d| ≥ 0.5) applied on top of FDR significance, to additionally require meaningful effect size, not just p-value significance at large n.
    4. **Zero-inflated Poisson (ZIP)** — attempted for the sparse gene+protein overlap burden; document convergence failure rate (~1/3 of genes) and the Hessian-inversion warnings as a explicit negative methodological result.
    5. **Two-part Hurdle model** (logistic "any outlier?" component + truncated Poisson "magnitude | outlier present" component) — your principal solution to the zero-inflation problem; explain the modeling logic and why it's more interpretable than ZIP for this sparse-count setting.
    6. **Plain logistic regression** on binary outlier presence — used both as a component of the hurdle model and standalone, interpreted via odds ratios (exp(coefficient)).
    7. **Negative-binomial GLM** (`statsmodels` `smf.glm`/`smf.negativebinomial`) — your main workhorse for burden ~ predictor regressions with covariates (diagnosis as categorical fixed effect `C(Diag)`, background CNV burden, other omic burdens); note where HC3/H3C robust standard errors were used and why (heteroskedasticity-robust inference for count model with possible misspecification), and where Poisson with robust SEs was substituted for negative binomial (Pol II underexpression final model).
    8. **Fisher's exact test** — gene-specific spliceosome small-variant analysis (2×2 contingency: carrier/outlier status), used because per-gene carrier counts were too small for a regression approach.
    9. **Permutation / empirical null tests** — (a) weighted resampling permutation test for individual trans-candidate splicing genes; (b) 10,000-iteration random-gene-panel permutation test to establish whether the Pol II gene set's effect size exceeds that of size-matched random gene sets (this is your specificity control — very important, foreground it in Methods and Results).
    10. **Pathway-agnostic scan** — Reactome pathway database (2,267 annotated pathways), per-pathway carrier-vs-burden GLM, with gene-set-size filters (5–200 genes), carrier-frequency filters (min carriers = 5, max = 15% of cohort) to avoid testing pathways that are too small/too ubiquitous, FDR correction, and **Jaccard-distance hierarchical clustering (average linkage, distance threshold 0.5)** of significant pathways' gene sets to collapse redundant/overlapping pathways into independent signal "nodes" (keeping the most significant/strongest-effect pathway per cluster) — explain this clustering step carefully, it's a nice methodological contribution worth its own paragraph.

## 5. Discussion

_(This is the section you specifically flagged as needing to be "very solid" — go finding-by-finding, and for each: (a) restate the result in one line, (b) give the strongest biologically plausible interpretation, (c) give at least one alternative/confounding explanation, (d) say what would resolve the ambiguity)_

- **5.1 Chromosome-awareness as a general principle for burden-based CNV analysis**
    - Interpretation: co-amplification via chromothripsis/local structural rearrangement systematically inflates naive burden-testing results for genes near true drivers; your method demonstrably increases both signal count _and_ enrichment for known cancer genes — argue this generalizes beyond your specific dataset as a methodological contribution.
    - Alternative explanation to consider: chromosome-aware exclusion could also _remove real_ trans-signal if a driver happens to share a chromosome with many of its true trans-targets by chance — discuss this as a conservative bias you accept, and quantify roughly how conservative (e.g., using the disproportionate chr12 significant-gene-count figure).
- **5.2 TSFM / CDK4 / MDM2 / AGAP2 / AGAP2-AS1**
    - CDK4: canonical, well-validated oncogene and G1/S cell-cycle driver, clinically actionable (CDK4/6 inhibitors) — treat as a positive control confirming your method works.
    - AGAP2 / AGAP2-AS1: currently only supported by GeneCards-level evidence (anti-apoptotic PI3K activation, invasion promotion for AGAP2; multi-cancer biomarker lncRNA for AGAP2-AS1) — explicitly flag this as **not yet primary-literature-verified** in your thesis and 🔎 find the primary papers behind these GeneCards claims before treating this as a confirmed finding.
    - TSFM: currently the most ambiguous hit — biologically plausible functional role (mitochondrial translation elongation factor Ts, partner of TUFM/mitochondrial EF-Tu) is _not_ an obvious cancer mechanism, and your own collinearity analysis (r=0.918 with MDM2 amplification, only 18 co-occurring samples) means you cannot currently rule out that it's a passenger riding the CDK4/MDM2 amplicon. Present this honestly as an open question, not a discovery — a great candidate for your Outlook's "functional validation" point. 🔎 Search specifically for TSFM + cancer / mitochondrial translation + oncogenesis literature.
- **5.3 Zero-inflation and the hurdle-model result**
    - Interpret the general UQCRH-style pattern (CNV raises _probability_ but not _magnitude_ of becoming a dual expression+protein outlier) mechanistically: consistent with a threshold/switch-like disruption (e.g., a CNV either pushes a gene's regulatory system past a stability threshold or it doesn't, rather than proportionally scaling dysregulation with "dose") — but caveat this interpretation clearly as speculative and state what data (e.g., copy-number _magnitude_, not just presence/absence) would test it directly.
    - Methodological reflection: discuss why ZIP failed to converge for ~1/3 of genes (likely near-complete separation / insufficient positive counts) and why the two-part hurdle model is more robust for sparse dual-omic overlap data generally — position this as advice for future similarly-structured analyses.
- **5.4 Minor spliceosome finding**
    - This is a genuinely novel angle if the literature doesn't already cover it in cancer — discuss what's biologically distinctive about minor-spliceosome-dependent introns (their number is small, ~700–800 in the human genome, but the genes containing them are reportedly enriched for specific functional categories — 🔎 verify and cite this precisely) and why disruption of a low-redundancy, high-precision machinery could plausibly create outsized global splicing noise compared to disrupting one arbitrary splicing factor.
    - Contrast with the SF3B1 example from your literature review (§2.5) — SF3B1 mutations are recurrent, hotspot, gain-of-function-like changes with tumor-type-specific splicing signatures, whereas your small-variant spliceosome carriers are a heterogeneous set of rare LoF-like variants — discuss why you'd expect a different (more diffuse, burden-based rather than signature-based) signal from the latter.
- **5.5 RNA Pol II defects and genome-wide underexpression**
    - Primary interpretive hypothesis: partial loss of core transcriptional machinery capacity plausibly reduces global transcription rate/efficiency in a dosage-sensitive way, producing many mild underexpression outliers rather than one strong local effect — this is the "trans" story your whole thesis is testing, so spend real space arguing for it.
    - Competing/confounding explanation you must address directly: could `has_pol2_underexp` just be a proxy for general chromosomal instability or aneuploidy (cells with more genomic damage broadly, including but not limited to Pol II genes, might show more global transcriptional noise for reasons unrelated to Pol II specifically)? You partially controlled for this via `cnv_zscore` and the random-gene-panel permutation test — explicitly argue why the permutation result (Pol II coefficient exceeding the null distribution of size-matched random gene sets) is your strongest evidence against this confound, but also acknowledge it doesn't fully rule out lineage-specific instability patterns correlated with Pol II gene location.
    - Discuss the asymmetry between under- and overexpression results (if overexpression effect was weaker/absent) — mechanistically, loss-of-function-style CNV/variant tiers on core transcriptional machinery genes would be expected to reduce, not increase, global output, so an asymmetric result would itself be a form of internal validation; if the effect was _not_ asymmetric, discuss what that would mean instead.
    - Emphasize the circularity-control step (removing Pol II genes' own outlier calls from the target variable) as a methodological strength worth explicit discussion — many burden analyses in the literature don't do this and risk trivial self-fulfilling results.
- **5.6 Convergent CDK4/CDK6 evidence across independent analyses**
    - This is your strongest overall result — argue explicitly for triangulation: an unbiased, hypothesis-free, genome-wide pathway scan (§4.5) recovering the _same_ 5-gene CDK4/CDK6 signal independently found via a targeted single-gene CNV burden test (§4.2) substantially raises confidence that this is real biology, not a statistical artifact of either method alone.
    - Connect to the clinical CDK4/6-inhibitor literature (Reactome pathway note on cytostatic effects and immune microenvironment modulation) to argue translational relevance — but 🔎 back this with a primary clinical/pharmacology reference, not only the Reactome pathway browser text.
- **5.7 Heterogeneity across cancer lineages (cis/trans breakdown, per-lineage Pol II and spliceosome effects)**
    - Discuss as evidence that "trans-regulatory disruption" is not a uniform pan-cancer phenomenon but is modulated by tissue/lineage context — a reasonable hypothesis is that lineages with characteristically unstable genomes (high background CNV burden, e.g., sarcomas) show more trans/inter-chromosomal-dominant patterns, while lineages with more stable genomes and known recurrent local drivers (e.g., specific fusion-driven sarcomas) show more cis-dominant patterns — 🔎 if you want to make this argument for a specific lineage (e.g., synovial sarcoma / SYNS), get the primary literature on that lineage's genomic architecture (e.g., SS18-SSX fusion biology) rather than speculating from the aggregate figure alone.
- **5.8 Limitations** (be direct and complete — examiners will ask about these)
    - Power: several key findings (TSFM standalone significance, per-lineage GLMs, rare gene-specific spliceosome tests) rest on small carrier counts (tens to low hundreds) — state this plainly per-finding, not just once generically.
    - Multiple testing across thousands of genes/pathways: FDR controls expected false-discovery proportion but doesn't guarantee any individual top hit is real — reiterate the CPG-literature caution that rarity/significance ≠ causal driver status.
    - Residual confounding by cancer lineage: `C(Diag)`/lineage covariates and background CNV burden z-scores reduce but don't eliminate confounding; per-lineage refits are themselves underpowered for rarer lineages.
    - Cross-sectional, observational design: no experimental perturbation, so all "drives"/"causes" language in your Results should really be framed as "is associated with" — be careful and consistent about causal language throughout the whole thesis, not just here.
    - Binary carrier/burden definitions understate biological gradation (e.g., no explicit CN-magnitude or zygosity modeling except for the coarse variant-tiering scheme) — note as a design choice with tradeoffs (simplicity/interpretability vs. granularity).
    - Chromosome-awareness is a heuristic spatial exclusion, not a formal causal/mediation model — note that it's a practical correction, not a proof of trans-mechanism.
