- Transcriptional addiction: dependencies on specific trans-regulators due to dysregulation of gene-expression (cancer relies on dysregulated and excessive transcription)
- Cancer cells disproportionately rely on a subset of regulators, eg super-enhancer associated factors 
- a small number of lineage regulators (master TFs) establish a cell's identity => organise super-enhancers, which are clusters of enhancers with higher densities of TFs, chromatin regulators, etc
- some key trans-regulatory drug targets include CDK7/8/9/12/13

- transcriptional addiction extends to 'oncogene addiction'
- near-universal amplification/ dysregulation of Myc => direct Myc inhibition is an unsolved problem which is why a known trans cofactor (BRD4) is targeted instead 
- key concept (case study BRD4): a trans-acting factor with genome-wide effects can produce gene-selective phenotypic effects because of differential super-enhancer architecture
- subconcepts: autoregulatory loops of master-TFs means that any dysruption is amplified => similarly cooperative binding of coactivators for super-enhancers creates a switch-like dose response resulting in a disproportionate transcriptional response:

Targeting strategies based on transcriptional addiction:
- direct inhibition of oncogenic TFs/cofactors (eg ATRA degrading PML-RARA, BET bromodomain inhibitors in BRD4-NUT midline carcinoma)
- targeting core regulatory circuitry (anti-CD20, CAR-T) => exploits dependency on lineage TFs that are not oncogenic in and of themselves 
- context-specific dependencies 
- targeted epigenetic editing of insulated neighbourhoods to modulate oncogene expression 
- targeted protein degradation ("degronimids")

- transcriptional dependencies (unlike oncogenic mutations) are not typically discoverable by genome sequencing but must be identified via mechanistic/ functional studies
