- Pseudogenes can be functionally active in cancer 
- act via RNA-RNA and RNA-protein interactions (trans-regulatory)
- Pseudogenes can be classified into three classes: processed, unprocessed, and unitary
- some pseudogenes have been shown to be exclusively expressed in cancer tissues (Kalyana-Sundaram et al.)
- Regulation of pseudogene expression: "hitchhiking" on host gene regulatory machinery when inserted into an intron, insertion into open/ actively transcribed chromatin regions, etc
- mechanisms of pseudogene trans function: endogenous competitors (transcripts compete with mRNAs for shared regulatory elements), hybridisation with coding genes (RNA-RNA duplexes), regulation of DNA structure (recruitment of epigenetic modifiers)
